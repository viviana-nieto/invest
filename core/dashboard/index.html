      How to read this dashboard

      ⚙ The deterministic engine makes every call; 🤖 the LLM agents only narrate and route. Every verdict, tier, and number here is plain arithmetic on the inputs — same inputs, same answer, every run. The italic one-liners marked 🤖 are commentary written after the fact; they never change a verdict.

      Two lenses, one loop. The Value lens asks "is this a good business at a fair price?" The Technicals lens asks "is now a sensible moment?" A name is most interesting when both agree.

      Value lens — is the business worth it?

      Payback Time (PBT) — how many years of the company's earnings, growing at its current rate, it would take to buy the whole company back at today's price. Fewer years = cheaper. The verdict uses < 12 years.

      Margin of Safety (MoS) — how far below fair "sticker" value the price sits. +30% means you would pay 30% less than the estimate of what the business is worth — room to be wrong.

      Free Cash Flow (FCF) — the real cash left over after running and maintaining the business. FCF yield divides that cash by the price you pay — like an interest rate on the purchase.

      BUY / WATCH / PASS — three checks: Payback < 12y, Margin of Safety > 0, FCF yield ≥ 0%. All 3 pass → BUY. 2 pass → WATCH. 1 or fewer → PASS.

      Screen — the stricter cut

      A tighter, high-conviction filter than the watchlist verdict: Payback ≤ 10y AND FCF yield ≥ 5%.

      Pass = clears both cuts. Watch = clears exactly one. Everything else drops out.

      Technicals — is now a sensible moment?

      Stochastic (14,5,3) — compares today's price to its recent range. A low reading that is turning up says "oversold and recovering".

      MACD (8,17,9) — compares a fast and a slow average of the price. When the gap between them turns up, downward momentum is fading.

      SMA — the simple 50-day average of the price. Sitting below it and reclaiming it is the classic "finding a floor" pattern.

      Channel (linreg) — a straight trend line fitted through the last year of prices, with a rail above and below. Near the lower rail = cheap versus its own trend; the upper rail = stretched. ⚠ flags names still high inside a multi-year run-up.

      Floor vs Ceiling — the same checks read in both directions. Floor = buy timing: near the lower rail while oversold and turning up (Stochastic under 20, MACD turning up, price reclaiming its 50-day average) — a decline running out of sellers. Ceiling = sell/trim timing: near the upper rail while overbought and rolling over (Stochastic over 80, MACD rolling over, price losing its 50-day average) — an advance running out of buyers. The Technicals tab folds this into one badge: REACHING FLOOR / NEUTRAL / REACHING CEILING.

      Tiers — each side's checks combine into 🟢 strong (≥2 confirmations), 🟡 setting up (1), ⚪ watching (0); at the ceiling the dots flip to 🔴/🟠 sell-side.

      The St·M·MA column — one glyph per check (Stochastic · MACD · MA) plus the tier dot, folded into a single signed score, so one click sorts strongest-sell → strongest-buy.

      Options — the pricing sandbox

      Spot vs Strike — spot is where the stock trades right now; strike is the fixed price the contract lets you buy (call) or sell (put) at, no matter what the stock does.

      Premium — what the option itself costs today.

      Delta (Δ) — how much the premium moves when the stock moves $1; also a rough "chance it finishes in the money".

      Intrinsic vs Time value — intrinsic is what the option would be worth if it expired this second; the rest of the premium is time value, paid for what could still happen before expiry.

      Break-even — strike + premium for a call (strike − premium for a put): where the stock must sit at expiry just to get your money back, also shown as the % move needed.

      Theta decay — time value melts away as expiry approaches, which is why "$85 today" and "$85 in six months" are worth different amounts. That is exactly what the ① Now vs ② Future what-if cards show.
